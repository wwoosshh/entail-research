BODY: ### Summary `_causal_conv1d_fwd_kernel` and `_causal_conv1d_update_kernel` let Triton specialize on the 16-byte alignment of their metadata pointers (`cache_indices_ptr`, `query_start_loc_ptr`, `conv_state_indices_ptr`, ...). Three facts make this a problem: 1. **The specialization buys nothing here.** These arrays are read one scalar at a time (a block index, an offset), so the aligned and unaligned binaries run at the same speed. Alignment specialization only pays off for wide vectorized loads (`x_ptr`, `w_ptr`, ... — not these args). 2. **Their alignment flips at runtime.** They are per-step slices of batch metadata, so the address offset depends on the batch layout of that step. 3. **Warmup can never cover the unaligned variant.** Warmup tensors are freshly allocated and therefore always aligned. So the first compile of the unaligned variant is guaranteed to happen *during inference* — this is (part of) the latency spike tracked in #43009, and `jit_monitor` (#45631) duly warns about it.
